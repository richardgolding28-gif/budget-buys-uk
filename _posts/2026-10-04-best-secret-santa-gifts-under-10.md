A £10 Secret Santa budget is tight enough that it's easy to default to whatever's nearest the till, and that usually shows. The gifts that actually land tend to be either genuinely useful, genuinely funny, or recognisable enough that nobody's disappointed opening them in front of the group. Here's what to look for, and five real options that cover a puzzle, a pamper set, a novelty item, a chocolate box and a classic game.

## What actually matters

- **Go for something recognisable over something obscure.** A well-known brand or classic item is a safer bet in front of a group than an unfamiliar novelty gift that might not land.
- **Consumables are the lowest-risk option.** Chocolate, sweets and similar treats get used regardless of someone's specific taste in colours, scents or hobbies.
- **Funny only works if you know the audience.** A novelty gift can be the best item in the exchange or the most awkward one, depending entirely on whether the group's sense of humour matches it.
- **Check it actually looks like £10, not £2.** Packaging and presentation matter more at this budget than at any higher one — a slightly nicer box or tin reads as more thoughtful for the same spend.
- **Something with repeat use beats a one-off.** A puzzle or card game that gets played with more than once feels more generous than a single-use novelty item at the same price.
